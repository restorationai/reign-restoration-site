## Restoration emergencies common in Murphy

Murphy sits in the Blackland Prairie region of Collin County, where heavy clay soil expands and contracts with rainfall and drought cycles. That movement stresses slab foundations and the plumbing running through and under them, which is a common source of slow leaks that go unnoticed until flooring or baseboards start showing damage. North Texas also swings between intense spring and early-summer storms, capable of dumping several inches of rain in an hour, and occasional hard winter freezes that have split copper and PEX lines in homes never designed for sustained sub-freezing temperatures. Add in the region's humidity for several months of the year, and any water intrusion that isn't dried quickly has a real head start toward mold growth, often within 24 to 48 hours.

Most of Murphy's housing stock was built from the 1990s through the 2010s as the city grew into a Dallas-area suburb, meaning slab-on-grade foundations, brick veneer over wood framing, and PEX or copper supply lines are the norm rather than the older galvanized or cast iron systems found in older North Texas towns.
