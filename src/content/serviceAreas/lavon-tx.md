## Restoration emergencies common in Lavon

Most of Lavon's growth has happened fast, and that pace shapes what goes wrong. New-construction slabs poured on Blackland Prairie clay shift as the clay expands and contracts with North Texas rain cycles, which stresses plumbing joints and can open hairline gaps where water tracks in along the foundation perimeter. Spring brings fast-moving storm cells off the Lake Lavon watershed, with hail and straight-line wind that strip shingles and drive water behind fascia and soffits before anyone notices a leak. Summers run hot and humid, so any water intrusion that sits even a day or two behind drywall or under flooring has a real head start toward mold colonization. Winter is shorter here than up north, but the occasional hard freeze still catches exposed supply lines and irrigation backflow devices off guard, and burst-pipe calls spike in the days after a cold snap breaks.

Much of Lavon's housing stock is recent, built during the area's rapid growth over the last two decades, which means slab-on-grade foundations, brick or fiber-cement veneer, and PEX supply lines are far more common here than the galvanized or cast-iron plumbing found in older North Texas towns.
