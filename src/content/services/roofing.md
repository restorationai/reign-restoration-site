A roof that's lost granules in patches, has soft spots near the valleys, or shows daylight through the attic decking isn't a repair problem anymore, it's a replacement decision. Shingles have a working life, and once a roof passes that point, every storm that rolls through North Texas finds the weak spots faster. Reign Restoration installs and replaces residential roofing systems for homeowners in Royse City who need a straight answer about whether to patch or rebuild, and a crew that can do either without guesswork.

## What roofing installation and replacement actually involves

A full roof replacement starts at the decking, not the shingles. Crews tear off the existing roofing material down to the sheathing, inspect the plywood or OSB underneath for rot and soft spots caused by long-term moisture intrusion, and replace any decking that won't hold a nail properly. From there it's underlayment, flashing at every penetration (chimneys, vents, skylights), ice and water shield in valleys, and then the finish material, whether that's architectural asphalt shingles, metal panels, or another system the homeowner has chosen. A typical single-family roof replacement in this area runs one to three days depending on square footage, roof pitch, and how much decking needs to be replaced. New roof installation on new construction or additions follows the same sequence but starts with bare sheathing instead of tear-off.

## Our process

1. **Roof inspection and scope.** We walk the roof and the attic, documenting shingle condition, decking integrity, flashing points, and ventilation. Hail bruising, wind-lifted shingles, and granule loss all get photographed for the written scope.
2. **Material selection and written estimate.** Homeowners choose shingle type, color, and any upgrades (ridge vent, synthetic underlayment) before work starts. The estimate is written, not verbal, so there's no ambiguity about what's included.
3. **Tear-off and decking repair.** Old roofing comes off down to the deck. Any plywood or OSB that's delaminated, soft, or rotted from past leaks gets cut out and replaced before anything new goes down.
4. **Underlayment, flashing, and installation.** Ice and water shield goes in valleys and around penetrations first, then synthetic underlayment, then the new roofing material installed to manufacturer specifications and local code.
5. **Final walkthrough and cleanup.** We check flashing, ridge lines, and ventilation, run a magnetic sweep for nails, and haul off the old material and debris.

## What separates a good roofing replacement from a bad one

The most common shortcut in roofing is layering new shingles over old ones instead of tearing off down to the deck. It's faster and cheaper, but it hides decking damage, voids most manufacturer warranties, and adds weight the structure wasn't built to carry long-term. Another frequent miss is flashing: reusing old step flashing or skipping new flashing at chimneys and skylights is a leading cause of leaks that show up months after installation, not immediately.

Insurance adjusters evaluating storm damage look closely at whether hail hits are true impact bruising (soft, circular, granule loss down to the mat) versus cosmetic wear, and whether the damage is concentrated on slopes facing the storm's direction. A roofing contractor who documents this correctly, with close-up photos and a clear slope-by-slope breakdown, gives the claim a better chance of holding up. One that eyeballs it from the ground and writes a generic estimate often gets the claim kicked back for more documentation.

## Seasonal and regional considerations

Rockwall County sees its share of spring and early summer hailstorms, and those are the events most likely to trigger a full roof replacement rather than a spot repair. Summer heat also plays a role: asphalt shingles installed or worked on during peak afternoon temperatures can scuff or seal improperly, so timing matters for a clean installation. Homeowners dealing with a roof that's aging out, rather than storm-damaged, often plan replacement in spring or fall when temperatures make for better shingle sealing and a more comfortable job site.
