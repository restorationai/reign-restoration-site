## What should you actually look for in a storm damage contractor?

Four things matter more than anything a sales pitch will tell you: a physical local office, a license you can verify, in-house roofing and interior restoration, and a willingness to document the damage before any repair starts. A contractor missing any one of these is a bigger risk than the hail damage itself.

Verify the license directly through the [Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/LicenseSearch/) rather than taking a truck decal's word for it. Ask who actually dries the structure if water got in behind the hail-damaged shingles: a roofer without water damage credentials may tarp the roof and leave the wet insulation and drywall for someone else to figure out weeks later.

## How can you tell a storm chaser from a local restoration company?

A storm chaser is a crew that travels from storm to storm, signs roofs on the spot, and often has no permanent local office to return to once the work is done. Local companies have a fixed address, a Texas license tied to that address, and a reason to still be answering the phone a year from now if a repair fails.

Watch for these specific patterns: door-to-door solicitation before you've filed a claim, pressure to sign an assignment-of-benefits form on the spot, a business card with only a cell number and no street address, and reluctance to put the scope of work in writing. The [Texas Department of Insurance](https://www.tdi.texas.gov/tips/storm-damage.html) publishes a consumer guide specifically warning Texans about this pattern after hail events, and it's worth a five-minute read before any contract gets signed.

## Does homeowners insurance cover storm damage restoration, and how do these companies help with the claim?

Most Texas homeowners policies cover sudden wind and hail damage to the roof and resulting interior water damage, subject to your deductible and any roof-age depreciation schedule written into the policy. What a policy pays out depends on your specific coverage, so the number on your declarations page is the only one that counts.

A restoration company's real value in a storm claim is documentation: photographing the hail hits and wind damage before any tarp goes up, measuring moisture intrusion with a meter, and writing a scope of work the adjuster can reference line by line. That paperwork is often the difference between a claim that gets approved as written and one that gets argued over for weeks. If you want a deeper walkthrough of the claims process itself, the site has a separate guide on [filing a water damage insurance claim in Texas](/blog/water-damage-insurance-claim/) that covers documentation requirements in more detail.
