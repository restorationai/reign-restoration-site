## Do you call your insurance company or a contractor first?

Call a licensed storm-response contractor before your insurance company, the moment water is actively getting into the house. A contractor can tarp the opening and start extracting water within the hour; most insurers don't even dispatch an adjuster that fast, and every hour water sits on drywall or subfloor adds to the loss.

Your policy almost certainly requires you to "mitigate further damage" after a covered loss, which is insurance language for stopping the bleeding. That's the tarp, the plywood, the extraction, the fans. Once the active damage is stopped and documented, that's when you file the claim. Calling the insurer first and waiting on a claim number before you tarp a hole is how a $4,000 roof repair turns into a $20,000 interior rebuild.

## What do you do in the first hour after a storm damages your roof?

In the first hour, get everyone away from sagging ceilings or exposed electrical, then stop water from entering further before you touch anything else. A sagging or stained ceiling can mean a saturated ceiling joist overhead; treat it like it could fail.

From there:

- Shut off power to any room with water near outlets or fixtures, at the breaker, not the switch.
- Photograph the damage from outside and inside before you move or cover anything, for the claim file.
- Move furniture, rugs and electronics out from under the leak if it's safe to do so.
- Get a tarp or plywood over the opening. If you can't do this safely yourself from the ground, that's exactly the call Reign Restoration's emergency crews handle, since roof access after a storm is a fall hazard most homeowners shouldn't take on with a ladder in wet conditions.

## Does a roofer or a restoration company handle storm damage?

A roofer handles the exterior repair, the decking and shingles; a restoration company handles everything the water touched once it got inside, and most homeowners end up needing both. If you call a roofer alone, you've fixed the hole but nobody is drying the wet insulation and drywall behind it, which is exactly the setup for hidden mold growth three weeks later.

This is why it's worth calling one contractor who runs both [storm damage restoration](/services/storm-damage-restoration/) and [roofing](/services/roofing/) crews rather than coordinating two separate companies who each blame the other for what the adjuster questions. **Should I call my insurance company before a restoration company?**
No. Call a contractor first if water is actively entering the home, since most policies require you to limit further damage and a contractor can tarp and extract within the hour. File the claim once the active loss is stopped and photographed.

**How much does emergency roof tarping cost after a storm?**
Emergency tarping itself usually runs a few hundred dollars for materials and labor on an average residential roof, and is typically covered as emergency mitigation under a storm claim once the full loss is documented. Full repair or replacement cost is scoped separately after the adjuster inspects.

**Will insurance cover a full roof replacement or just a tarp?**
It depends on the inspection and your policy's wind/hail provisions. A tarp is almost always covered as temporary mitigation; a full replacement depends on whether the adjuster finds the damage extensive enough to meet your policy's repair-versus-replace threshold, which a written contractor scope helps support.
