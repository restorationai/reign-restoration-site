## Is it a roofer or a mold remediation company you need first?

If water is actively coming through the roof deck, call about the roof first; if the leak is already stopped and you're just looking at staining or growth, call about the mold first. Either way, the two jobs are connected and often handled by the same crew.

North Texas attics take a beating from hail and straight-line wind that Royse City, Rockwall, and Fate see most springs. A cracked shingle or a lifted ridge cap doesn't always leak right away. It can take several storms, and several months, before water finds its way to the decking and starts wicking into insulation and framing. By the time anyone notices a ceiling stain or a musty smell pulling down from the attic hatch, mold has usually had the 24 to 48 hours it needs on wet material to get started, per the [EPA's guidance on mold and moisture](https://www.epa.gov/mold/brief-guide-mold-moisture-and-your-home).

That's the reason a lot of homeowners end up making two calls when one would have worked: a roofer who patches the leak but doesn't touch the mold, and a mold company that can't guarantee the fix because the leak source is somebody else's scope. A single company that does both, as Reign Restoration does, can inspect the roof and the attic in the same visit and write one scope instead of two.

## What happens when you call about attic mold?

The first call is a short phone triage to figure out if the leak is active, how large the stained or affected area looks, and whether anyone has spent time in the attic breathing the air directly. From there it's an in-person inspection before any work starts.

On-site, a technician checks the roof deck from inside the attic for the actual entry point, not just the stained drywall below it. They'll use a moisture meter on the rafters and sheathing to map how far the water traveled, and look for visible growth on wood, insulation paper facing, or ductwork. If the growth looks limited to a small, accessible area, a surface assessment may be enough to scope the work. If it's spread across a larger section of decking or trusses, third-party air or surface sampling is sometimes recommended before remediation, so the scope is based on lab results rather than a guess.

Have ready: when you first noticed the smell or stain, whether the leak is still active, and any recent roof damage you already know about (a hailstorm date helps a lot). Remediation itself follows the [ANSI/IICRC S520 standard](https://iicrc.org/s520/): containment is set up to isolate the attic access point, affected insulation and any porous material that can't be cleaned is removed and bagged, exposed wood is HEPA-vacuumed and treated, and the space is run through negative air filtration before anyone calls it done. The roof repair, when it's part of the same job, happens before the attic is closed back up, not after.

## Does insurance cover mold in the attic after a roof leak?

Most homeowners policies cover mold removal when it results from a sudden, covered event, like a storm-damaged roof that let water in, but not when the cause is a long-term leak nobody addressed. The distinction between those two is exactly what the adjuster will be looking at.

If hail or wind damaged the roof and that damage is documented (date of storm, photos of the shingles, a roofing inspection report), the resulting mold is often treated as part of the storm claim. If the attic has clearly been wet for a long time, with widespread staining, rotted sheathing, or growth that looks established rather than recent, insurers often push back, arguing the homeowner should have caught and fixed the leak sooner. This is why a prompt inspection matters more than most homeowners expect, and why documentation from the first visit, not the fourth, protects the claim. For general rules on what's typically covered, the [Insurance Information Institute's guide to homeowners coverage](https://www.iii.org/article/what-is-covered-by-standard-homeowners-insurance) is a useful starting point, though every policy is different and the actual terms control.

## What does attic mold removal typically cost in Royse City?

Most attic mold jobs in this area run somewhere between a few hundred dollars for a small, contained spot and several thousand for a larger area that involves insulation removal and roof repair together. The number depends heavily on square footage affected and whether the roof leak itself still needs fixing.

Small, isolated growth on a single rafter or small patch of decking, caught early, is the cheapest scenario because there's little to remove beyond the affected surface. A larger spread across trusses and insulation, especially if soaked batt insulation has to come out and be replaced, costs more and takes longer. Combining the roof repair with the remediation in one scope, rather than hiring two separate contractors, usually comes out ahead because there's no duplicated setup or teardown.
