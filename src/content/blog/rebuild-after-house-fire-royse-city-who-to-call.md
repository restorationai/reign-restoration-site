## What order should you make these calls in?

The order protects your claim and your rebuild timeline: fire department first, insurer second, board-up third, then restoration and reconstruction. Skipping a step, or doing them out of order, is the most common reason a fire claim gets delayed or partially denied.

1. **Fire department / fire marshal.** Nobody re-enters until the responding department or the local fire marshal releases the structure. In Royse City that release, and any fire investigation report, becomes part of your insurance file.
2. **Your insurance company.** Report the loss the same day if you can. Ask for your claim number and, if the home is unlivable, ask about Additional Living Expenses (ALE) coverage for temporary housing. The [Texas Department of Insurance](https://www.tdi.texas.gov/) publishes consumer guidance on what a homeowners policy typically covers after a disaster and how the claims process works in this state.
3. **Board-up and tarp, same day.** A damaged roof, blown-out windows, or a hole from the fire department's ventilation work leaves the house open to weather and to theft. This gets secured within hours, not days, before anyone starts talking about rebuild plans.
4. **Fire and smoke cleanup, before any rebuild work starts.** Soot is acidic and keeps etching glass, metal, and finishes the longer it sits. [Smoke damage restoration](/services/smoke-damage-restoration/) and structural cleaning happen before drywall, paint, or flooring go back in, following the [ANSI/IICRC S700 Standard for Fire and Smoke Damage Restoration](https://iicrc.org/s700/).
5. **Contents pack-out and storage, in parallel.** Furniture, clothing, and belongings that can be saved get inventoried, packed, and moved to a [contents restoration](/services/contents-restoration/) facility for cleaning and smoke-odor treatment while the structure is worked on.
6. **Reconstruction, with City of Royse City permits pulled before framing starts.** Structural rebuild, drywall, and finishes come last, once the space is clean and dry, and once permits are in hand from the city's building department for anything beyond cosmetic repair.

## How long does it take to rebuild after a house fire?

Most single-family fire rebuilds in North Texas run eight to sixteen weeks from the day cleanup starts, though a kitchen fire contained to one room can close out faster and a whole-house loss can run several months. The timeline depends on how much structure burned, how long the insurance scope takes to finalize, and how fast permits clear the City of Royse City.

Three things stretch that timeline the most: waiting on an insurance adjuster to approve the full scope before any demolition starts, discovering hidden damage once drywall comes down (scorched framing, melted wiring, water damage from firefighting efforts), and permit review on larger structural work. A single company handling cleanup, documentation, and [reconstruction](/services/reconstruction/) under one roof tends to move faster than juggling separate vendors for each phase, because nobody is waiting on someone else's report to start their part.

## What happens when you call a restoration company first?

The first call is a walkthrough to document the damage before anything is touched. A technician photographs every affected room, checks for Category 2 or 3 water from firefighting hoses that can complicate drying, and writes a scope that becomes the basis for the insurance estimate. That documentation is what keeps a claim from getting argued down later.

From there, board-up and tarping get scheduled immediately if the structure is exposed, HEPA air scrubbers go in to start pulling soot particulate and odor out of the air, and salvageable contents get tagged for pack-out. Lingering smoke odor, especially in soft goods and HVAC ductwork, often needs a separate [odor removal](/services/odor-removal/) pass once the structural cleaning is done, since soot smell can hide in places a surface clean doesn't reach.
