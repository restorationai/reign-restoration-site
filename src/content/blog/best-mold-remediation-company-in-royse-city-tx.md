## What Should You Actually Check Before Hiring?

The single most useful filter is whether the company is set up to remediate under IICRC S520, not just clean visible mold off a surface. Ask whether the technician on your job holds [IICRC AMRT certification](https://www.iicrc.org/), whether the scope includes containment and negative air pressure for anything beyond a small, isolated patch, and whether a post-remediation clearance check is included before they call the job finished.

In Texas, mold remediation companies and individual remediators working on projects larger than 25 contiguous square feet must be licensed through the [Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation's Mold Assessors and Remediators program](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/mld/). You can confirm any company's license status directly through the [TDLR license search tool](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/LicenseSearch/) before you sign anything. Reign Restoration's license number is MRC2276.

If you're not sure whether you're looking at surface mold or something more serious, our guide on [how to tell black mold from regular mold](/blog/black-mold-vs-regular-mold/) walks through the visual differences, and [how to test for mold](/blog/how-to-test-for-mold/) covers DIY kits versus a professional air-quality assessment.

Mold can begin growing on wet building materials within 24 to 48 hours, according to the [EPA's guidance on mold and moisture](https://www.epa.gov/mold/brief-guide-mold-moisture-and-your-home), which is why the company's response time matters almost as much as its certifications once you've confirmed a moisture source.

If the growth traces back to a sudden, accidental event, like a burst supply line that got discovered late, your homeowners policy may cover a portion of the remediation. Gradual leaks and long-term moisture problems are typically excluded.
