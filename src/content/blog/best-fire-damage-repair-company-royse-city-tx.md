## What Should a Fire Damage Repair Company Be Certified In?

A qualified fire damage repair company holds IICRC Fire and Smoke Restoration Technician (FSRT) certification and follows the [ANSI/IICRC S700 Standard for Professional Fire and Smoke Damage Restoration](https://iicrc.org/s700/). That standard governs how soot is categorized, how odor is neutralized, and what documentation protects your insurance claim.

FSRT training covers something homeowners usually don't know to ask about: smoke behaves differently depending on what burned. Protein fires (a grease fire that never fully ignited) leave a nearly invisible residue that smells far worse than it looks. Synthetic fires (furniture, electronics, carpet) leave a sticky, acidic soot that etches metal and glass if it sits too long. A technician without FSRT training may clean the visible damage and miss the odor source entirely, which means you're back on the phone in three weeks.

Ask any company you're considering for their certification and license number before the first truck shows up, not after.

## What Happens in the First 24 Hours After a House Fire in Royse City?

The first 24 hours should cover securing the structure, documenting the damage, and starting moisture and odor control before soot sets further into porous materials. That window matters because soot residue becomes harder to remove the longer it sits on drywall, grout, and fabric.

A company equipped for this works in roughly this order: board up broken windows and any compromised exterior openings, tarp a damaged roof section if firefighting operations opened it up, photograph every room for the insurance file, and begin HEPA air scrubbing to pull soot particulate and odor out of the air before it resettles. If water was used to fight the fire, which is nearly always the case, extraction and drying start the same day the structure is cleared for entry.

## Does the Company Rebuild, or Does It Hand You Off to Someone Else?

The best fire damage repair company carries the job from initial mitigation through reconstruction without handing you off to a separate general contractor mid-project. That continuity matters because a handoff usually means a new scope, a new estimate, and a new wait for the second company's schedule to open up.

Many restoration outfits stop at cleanup and odor removal, then refer you out for the drywall, paint, flooring, and cabinetry work that gets you back to a livable house. That's not wrong, but it adds a seam where your claim can stall while two companies coordinate (or don't). A company that also holds reconstruction and general contracting capability can write one scope of work, bill it under one claim number, and keep the same project manager on the job from the smoke-damaged walk-through to the final punch list.

Reign Restoration's service line covers fire damage restoration, smoke damage restoration, odor removal, and [reconstruction](/services/reconstruction/) under one roof, which means the same team that documents the soot damage is the one framing the new drywall. If a company you're vetting can't answer "who does the rebuild" without naming a subcontractor you've never heard of, ask for that subcontractor's license and insurance before you sign anything.

## What Questions Should You Ask Before Signing a Fire Restoration Contract?

Before signing, ask for a written, itemized scope of work, direct confirmation of the IICRC certifications held by the technicians (not just the company name), and clarity on who handles contents (furniture, electronics, textiles) versus structure. Those three answers tell you most of what you need to know.

Specifically, ask:

- What is your IICRC FSRT and Certified Firm status, and can I see the certificate?
- What is your Texas license number, and is it active? (Check it yourself at [TDLR's license search](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/LicenseSearch/).)
- Will you work directly with my insurance adjuster, and do you provide documentation formatted for a claim?
- Does your scope include reconstruction, or will I need a separate contractor for drywall, flooring, and paint?
- What happens to smoke-damaged contents, furniture, and electronics? Are they cleaned on-site, or removed to a separate facility?
- What's the estimated timeline from mitigation to move-back-in, in writing?
