## Why Murphy Properties See Water Damage Issues

Most homes in Murphy were built from the late 1990s through the 2000s, during the city's fast suburban growth, which means a lot of slab-on-grade construction with PEX or copper supply lines run through the foundation. That construction style is efficient, but it also means a pinhole leak under a slab doesn't always announce itself with a puddle. It shows up as a warm spot on tile, a spike in the water bill, or a faint mildew smell near an interior wall. Add in Collin County's clay-heavy soil, which contracts hard during summer drought and swells fast when heavy spring storms roll through, and you get foundation movement that stresses plumbing joints year after year. Add a washing machine supply line failure or a water heater past its service life in a garage or utility closet, and that same clay soil can also slow how quickly groundwater drains away from a home's perimeter after a hard rain, which matters when a roof or window leak tracks moisture toward an exterior wall.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Murphy

The first visit is about stopping the loss and mapping what's wet, not guessing. We extract standing water with truck-mounted or portable extraction units, then use moisture meters and often a thermal camera to check how far water traveled under flooring and inside wall cavities, since Murphy's slab foundations tend to wick moisture sideways rather than pooling visibly. Engineered hardwood and laminate, both common in Murphy's newer builds, react differently than solid wood or tile. Laminate usually has to come up once it's been wet for more than a few hours because the core swells and won't reseat, while engineered hardwood sometimes dries in place if we get to it fast with the right airflow and dehumidification. Structural drying follows IICRC S500 guidelines, and we track moisture content daily with documented readings so the job closes out when the materials are actually dry, not just when they look dry on the surface.
